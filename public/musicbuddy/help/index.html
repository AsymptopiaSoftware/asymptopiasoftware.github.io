Musical reference points worth remembering while exploring journeys and Cosmic Harmony. Deliberately short; additions are made by request.

Bigger-picture insights

Relationships to recognize and hear, not more formulas to memorize.

Any minor seventh chord can begin a major ii–V–I

Move each root counterclockwise one step around the Circle of Fifths (up a fourth). The chord qualities are minor 7 → dominant 7 → major 7.

D-7 → G7 → CM7

Fifths and fourths use the same circle

Clockwise moves by fifths; counterclockwise moves by fourths. The roots of a ii–V–I follow the latter direction.

G → C → F gives G-7 → C7 → FM7

Transpose the roots, preserve the relationships

Changing the key of a journey moves its chord roots while retaining its relative chord qualities and harmonic functions. That's why the unlabeled Cosmic Harmony graph doesn't change when the selected key changes.

Tritone substitution: two dominants, shared guide tones

A dominant 7 chord can sometimes be replaced by another dominant 7 whose root lies a tritone away. Both share the same third/seventh guide-tone pair (enharmonically).

D-7 → G7 → CM7   /   D-7 → D♭7 → CM7

Small glossary

Only terms we've recently needed in practice.

C♯ø7 · half-diminished seventh

Also C♯-7♭5: minor third, diminished fifth, minor seventh above the root.

C♯ E G B

Chord labels: -7, 7, M7

G-7 is minor seventh (G B♭ D F); G7 is dominant seventh (G B D F); GM7 is major seventh (G B D F♯).

Secondary function: V7/iv

Read “five-seven of minor four.” In C major, iv is F minor, so its dominant is C7. The original tonic root C takes on a different harmonic function.

Gø7 → C7 → F-7   (iiø7/iv → V7/iv → iv)
